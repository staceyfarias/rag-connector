The Lunar Golf Links is a three-hole course laid across the Fra Mauro highland basin near the Apollo 14 landing site. It is the only sanctioned golf course on the Moon and one of the most distinctive sporting experiences in the solar system. The course operates under the management of the Fra Mauro Excursion Authority and is accessible to Pelorus Voyages passengers holding Navigator or Pathfinder cabin class, or through add-on purchase for Pioneer passengers.

Playing conditions are unlike any course on Earth. The Moon's gravity is approximately one-sixth of Earth's, which means a well-struck drive — identical swing, identical club, identical ball — will travel roughly 1.2 kilometres. The first hole plays 800 metres to the green; the approach shot on a typical par-four Earth course becomes the tee shot here. The ball follows a long, slow arc with no atmospheric drag or wind to redirect it. Reading lies is the primary skill; the regolith has texture ranging from compacted dust to loose granular material, and ball behaviour on the surface varies accordingly.

All rounds are played in modern articulated surface suits, which allow a full two-handed swing. This is a significant improvement over Alan Shepard's 1971 one-handed shots, which he managed in the rigid A7L suit with a makeshift club head attached to a contingency sample scoop. The Shepard Shot Marker — the locations of Shepard's original impacts — is flagged within the Fra Mauro heritage boundary approximately 400 metres south of the first tee. Approach within 20 metres of the marker is prohibited; the marker is visible from the tee box.

Equipment is provided: a set of six clubs and two balls per player are included in the excursion price. Personal clubs may be brought from Earth and count against the passenger's personal mass allowance. Any club brought from Earth must be declared at Moon Base 7 check-in for pressurisation safety assessment before being taken to the surface. Lost balls are not replaced mid-round; bring your own spares if you anticipate losing one. The course does not retrieve balls from regolith craters deeper than two metres.

Last tee times for the morning round are 1100. Passengers on the Grand Solar Tour layover at Moon Base 7 are strongly advised to book the morning round. The afternoon round's last tee time is 1500, and an 18:30 departure requires muster at 90 minutes before, meaning any round booked after 1230 creates a time margin too tight to recommend. The golf excursion booking confirmation will display a recommended latest tee time based on your departure window.

Communication between the vessel and Earth is governed by the speed of light, which cannot be exceeded regardless of drive technology. The delay between sending a message and receiving a response depends entirely on how far the vessel is from Earth at the time of transmission.

During the Earth-to-Moon transit, the one-way light delay is 1.3 seconds — negligible for all practical purposes. Voice calls, video, and real-time messaging function normally throughout the lunar leg and during the Moon Base 7 layover.

During the Mars leg, one-way light delay ranges from approximately 3 to 22 minutes depending on where Earth and Mars are in their respective orbits. Round-trip response time is 6 to 44 minutes. Voice calls are possible in a technical sense but practically uncomfortable — most passengers and their Earth contacts adapt to asynchronous message exchange. The vessel operates a message relay system that queues and transmits messages at scheduled uplink windows; messages to Earth are typically delivered within one to two hours of composition regardless of transmission window timing.

During the Venus leg, one-way delay ranges from 2 to 15 minutes. Venus orbits closer to the Sun than Earth, meaning the delay is sometimes shorter than the Mars leg and sometimes similar depending on alignment. Conditions at Venus are generally slightly better for communication than Mars.

During the transit to and from Ceres and the outer system, one-way delay ranges from 4 to 84 minutes. At Saturn, one-way delay is 43 to 84 minutes, making round-trip response times of 86 to 168 minutes the norm. Real-time conversation is not feasible. Passengers communicating with Earth from the Saturn leg should expect asynchronous exchange with reply windows of 2 to 3 hours minimum.

The vessel's communication bandwidth is shared across all passengers. During high-demand periods — major Earth news events, family milestones — the system queues messages and delivers them in order. Video messages are supported but are lower priority than text; during congested periods, video delivery may lag by several additional hours. Video calling in real-time is not supported beyond the lunar leg.

Passengers may bring personal satellite communication devices; these are not connected to the vessel's relay system and must operate independently. Personal devices must comply with the vessel's radio frequency allocation policy and be declared at Earth check-in. Personal devices operating on unauthorised frequencies will be deactivated for the duration of the voyage.

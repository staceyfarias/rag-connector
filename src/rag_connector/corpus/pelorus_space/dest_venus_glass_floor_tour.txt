The Glass Floor Tour is a 90-minute guided experience along the underside observation corridor of The Aphrodite. A pressurised walkway runs the full length of the station's lower hull, its floor made of 4-centimetre borosilicate glass panels rated to withstand the pressure differential and the mechanical loads of the drifting station. Below the glass: 52 kilometres of Venusian atmosphere, the upper cloud layer churning in gold and amber, and far below it the faint orange glow of the surface. The guide narrates the view throughout — cloud chemistry, the lightning patterns in the lower deck, what the surface conditions actually are — and stops at four viewing stations where the glass is widest.

Most passengers stop walking about halfway across. The visual disconnect between standing normally in a pressurised, well-lit corridor and looking directly through the floor into a planetary atmosphere is not something photographs prepare you for. A few passengers lie flat on the glass to eliminate the peripheral frame of the corridor walls. The guide is accustomed to both reactions and will hold the pace to whatever the group needs.

The minimum age for the Glass Floor Tour is 8 years old. Passengers under 18 must have a parent or guardian complete the waiver on their behalf. All passengers must sign the waiver regardless of age; it covers the standard liability acknowledgment and confirms the passenger has been briefed on emergency protocols in the event of a panel integrity alert. The tour runs in groups of up to 12 passengers and advance booking is required. In peak season — which includes the preferred departure windows during northern hemisphere summer — the Glass Floor Tour fills three weeks in advance. Booking through the Pelorus Voyages portal is strongly recommended immediately after voyage confirmation.

The Glass Floor Tour is included in the Navigator and Pathfinder cabin class fare. Pioneer class passengers purchase it as an add-on at the standard excursion rate. The tour is not the same product as the gondola descent; the two are booked separately and one does not substitute for the other.

Footwear with enclosed toes is required. Open-toed shoes and sandals are not permitted on the glass corridor due to the grip surface design of the panels. The corridor operates at standard atmospheric temperature; no special clothing is needed beyond what passengers would wear in any other part of the station.

The Terraforming Operations Zone is an active working site approximately 40 kilometres northwest of Mars Colony 1. Pelorus Voyages operates guided tours of the zone for Navigator and Pathfinder class passengers; Pioneer passengers may purchase the tour as an add-on. Transportation from MC-1 is by pressurised transport rover; the journey takes approximately 35 minutes each way.

The terraforming project at this site focuses on atmospheric seeding: a series of large-scale aerosol dispersal installations pump catalytic compounds into the lower Martian atmosphere to gradually thicken it and raise the surface temperature. The process is measured in decades and centuries, not years, and the effect of any single visit on the long-term atmospheric profile is effectively zero — visitors are not observing dramatic change but rather the scale and organisation of an engineering effort intended to outlast any individual human lifetime. The interpretive centre at the entrance to the zone puts the timescale in context and explains what the atmospheric readings from the monitoring stations actually mean.

Beyond the interpretive centre, the tour enters the active installation area. An EVA helmet is required in all zones beyond the interpretive centre, including the partially pressurised greenhouse dome sections. This is not immediately obvious to visitors expecting a fully enclosed environment in the domes; the domes are pressurised to approximately 0.3 Earth atmospheres, which supports plant growth experiments but is not breathable by humans without a helmet. The suit required is the standard MC-1 surface suit; no additional equipment beyond the helmet is needed in the dome sections. Attempting to remove the helmet inside the domes is a safety violation and will result in immediate excursion termination.

The greenhouse dome sections contain active biological material: engineered cyanobacterial cultures and early-stage vascular plant experiments. Photography is permitted but flash photography within the dome is prohibited to avoid disrupting the controlled light-cycle experiments. Passengers may not touch any plant material, culture vessel, or monitoring equipment.

The return journey to MC-1 must depart the Operations Zone no later than 60 minutes before the MC-1 Airlock Bay B check-in deadline. The tour coordinator manages this timing; passengers should not make independent arrangements to extend their time at the zone.

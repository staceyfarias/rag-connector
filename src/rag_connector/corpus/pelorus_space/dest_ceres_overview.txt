Ceres Waystation is a six-hour stop on the Outer Reaches Tour and Grand Solar Tour, positioned between the inner solar system legs and the Saturn approach. Ceres itself is the largest body in the main asteroid belt, technically classified as a dwarf planet at approximately 940 kilometres in diameter. It is the only dwarf planet in the inner solar system and the closest dwarf planet to Earth. The Ceres Waystation is a permanent commercial and scientific installation operated by the Asteroid Belt Authority; Pelorus Voyages is one of several expedition operators with licensed access.

The six-hour stop is a brief layover relative to other destinations. Passengers should understand before arriving that Ceres is not a multi-day destination — the stop is structured around one or two focused experiences rather than a broad exploration of the site. The two main excursions available are the Surface Gravity Walk (including the Occator Crater Rim tour option) and The Leap at the dedicated jump platform. These can be combined in a single six-hour stop by most passengers, but the timing is tight if both are desired; see the check-in requirements for the specific deadlines.

The Waystation Hub itself is a pressurised facility with a commercial concourse, a restaurant serving local and transported food, a communications relay centre, and a small exhibition on asteroid belt geology and the history of Ceres exploration. The exhibition is freely accessible to all Pelorus Voyages passengers during the stop and takes approximately 45 minutes to walk through. It includes the original Dawn spacecraft mission results and the subsequent discoveries about Ceres's subsurface ocean and the Occator bright deposits.

Ceres's distance from the Sun — approximately 2.7 to 3.0 AU depending on orbital position — means sunlight is significantly weaker than at Earth. The surface is dark and cold, and the sky as seen from the surface contains no bright planets visible to the naked eye that are not familiar from Earth. Jupiter is visible and distinctly non-stellar in appearance; Saturn is a bright point. The Milky Way at this distance from the Sun is visible in a way that it is not from the inner solar system.

The Waystation's position as the outer system's main resupply and emergency hub means it has capabilities beyond its size: a full medical facility, emergency docking infrastructure, and a communications relay that provides the best transmission windows of any stop on the ORT or GST itinerary.

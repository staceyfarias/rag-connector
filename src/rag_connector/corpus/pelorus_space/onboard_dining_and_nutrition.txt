Dining on a Pelorus Voyages vessel is structured differently by cabin class. All passengers eat well; the differences are in flexibility and setting rather than food quality.

Pioneer class passengers dine in the main dining hall on a fixed schedule: breakfast from 0700 to 0900, lunch from 1200 to 1400, and dinner from 1800 to 2000 UTC. The main dining hall operates cafeteria-style for breakfast and lunch and table service for dinner. The menu rotates on a seven-day cycle; a full week's menu is posted in the passenger portal and updated by day three of each transit leg to reflect whatever fresh produce was loaded at the last stop. Pioneer passengers may not order to their cabin and do not have access to the à la carte dining room.

Navigator class passengers have flexible access to the main dining hall at any service time — they are not restricted to fixed windows — and may additionally order selected items for cabin delivery during dining hall hours. Cabin delivery takes approximately 20 to 30 minutes. Navigator passengers also have access to the Navigator dining lounge, a smaller, quieter space adjacent to the main hall with a more limited but consistently available menu.

Pathfinder class passengers dine in the private dining room and may order from a full à la carte menu at any hour, not just during service times. A dedicated steward is available around the clock for Pathfinder cabin requests. The Pathfinder menu is prepared separately from the main dining service and uses a higher proportion of the fresh produce and proteins loaded at destination stops.

Nutritional considerations in low gravity are taken seriously by the galley team. The sustained 0.15g transit environment means the body requires fewer calories than at Earth gravity; the default portion sizes across all dining options are calibrated for the transit gravity level and increase slightly during stays at higher-gravity stops (Mars at 0.38g, Venus at 0.88g). Passengers who feel they need more food at any point are encouraged to ask; the galley does not restrict quantities.

Dietary requirements must be registered in the passenger portal before departure. Pelorus Voyages accommodates vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal, kosher, and most medical dietary requirements. Food allergies should be registered as allergies specifically, not as preferences — the galley handles registered allergy passengers under a separate preparation protocol. Passengers with severe allergies are encouraged to bring supplementary snacks in their personal baggage as an additional precaution.

Fresh produce varies by destination. The most notable additions to the menu come at Mars Colony 1, where the hydroponics programme produces leafy greens, tomatoes, and herbs that are loaded fresh for the Mars-to-Venus transit leg. Passengers often describe the Mars-grown salad on the first transit day after MC-1 as one of the best meals of the voyage.

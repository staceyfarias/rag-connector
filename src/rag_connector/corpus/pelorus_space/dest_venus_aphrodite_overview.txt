The Aphrodite is a floating resort station positioned at 52 kilometres altitude in the Venusian atmosphere, within the upper cloud deck. It is the only stop on any Pelorus Voyages itinerary where gravity closely matches Earth — the surface gravity of Venus is 0.9g, and at cloud deck altitude the effective gravity experienced on the station is approximately 0.88g. For most passengers, who will have spent the preceding days or weeks in microgravity transit and at Mars's 0.38g, arriving at The Aphrodite feels unexpectedly heavy. A brief wellness orientation is provided to all passengers on arrival, and 24 to 48 hours of mild fatigue is normal as the body readjusts. This is not a medical event and does not require a report to the vessel's medical officer unless accompanied by other symptoms.

The station drifts with the Venusian atmosphere's superrotating wind patterns, which travel at approximately 300 kilometres per hour at cloud deck altitude and complete a full circuit of the planet every four Earth days. The Aphrodite does not fight the wind — it is designed to drift with it. Over a standard three-night stay, the station travels roughly one-third of the way around Venus. The view from the observation decks changes continuously; no two viewing periods offer the same angle on the cloud formations below.

Two views define The Aphrodite experience and exist in striking contrast. Looking upward from the observation decks, passengers see clear sky: the sun, visibly larger and approximately 1.9 times brighter than from Earth, stars at night unobscured by atmosphere above the cloud deck. Looking downward — through the observation windows, the glass floor corridor, or during the gondola descent — passengers see the upper cloud layer in shades of gold, amber, and deep orange, lit from below by a dim reddish glow from the surface 52 kilometres below. Lightning flickers in the lower cloud layers. The surface temperature is 465 degrees Celsius and the atmospheric pressure is 90 times Earth's. The most hostile planetary surface in the solar system is directly below the pool deck.

The Aphrodite is the only destination in the solar system where the combination of Earth-like gravity, breathable atmosphere inside the station, and dramatic external environment creates conditions suitable for genuine resort amenities. There is a full-depth pool, sun deck, aqua volleyball court, paddleboard rental, and a floating bar. Sunbathing is possible and popular; the UV management system on the sun deck filters solar radiation to Earth-normal levels, though the increased solar intensity at Venus latitude means SPF 50+ sunscreen is provided as standard.

The resort is exclusively accessible to Inner Planets Survey and Grand Solar Tour passengers. Outer Reaches Tour passengers do not visit Venus.

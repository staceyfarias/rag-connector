The Gondola Descent is a separate experience from the Glass Floor Tour and must be booked independently. It involves boarding a pressurised observation pod — the gondola — that detaches from The Aphrodite's lower hull and descends approximately three kilometres into the Venusian upper cloud layer. The descent takes about 15 minutes; passengers spend roughly 45 minutes inside the cloud layer before the gondola ascends and redocks. Total duration from boarding to exit is approximately 90 minutes.

Inside the cloud layer, visibility drops. The sulphuric acid aerosol in the Venusian clouds creates a haze that ranges from translucent to near-opaque depending on cloud density at the time of descent. The gondola windows fog and clear in cycles as the pod moves through regions of varying density. The temperature outside the gondola during the descent reaches approximately 75 degrees Celsius, which is visible as a slight shimmer on the panel seals. Passengers feel none of this; the gondola's interior is maintained at standard cabin temperature. The experience is not dramatic in the cinematic sense — it is quiet, slightly disorienting, and unlike anything else on the itinerary.

The Gondola Descent is an add-on at all cabin classes; it is not included in any base fare. Pathfinder class passengers receive priority booking access, which means their booking window opens 48 hours before Navigator and Pioneer passengers. The gondola has a maximum of eight passengers per descent. Demand consistently exceeds capacity at popular departure windows; passengers interested in the gondola should book as early as their priority window allows.

The minimum age for the Gondola Descent is 16 years old. This is a hard limit and is not subject to waiver or parental consent override. Additionally, a separate medical clearance is required for the gondola beyond the standard voyage medical clearance. The gondola clearance specifically assesses cardiovascular response to the pressure changes during descent and must be obtained from the same physician who issued the standard clearance, or from a Pelorus Voyages-approved flight surgeon. The gondola clearance can be issued at the same physician appointment as the standard clearance if the appointment is scheduled with this in mind.

The gondola must begin its return ascent no later than 90 minutes before the vessel's departure window. The gondola operator enforces this independently of Pelorus Voyages; if the descent timeline approaches the 90-minute mark, the gondola will begin ascent whether or not passengers are ready. This is non-negotiable for trajectory scheduling reasons. Passengers who are considering both the Glass Floor Tour and the Gondola Descent on the same Venus stay should plan their schedules carefully; the combination is achievable but requires advance booking of both with attention to timing.

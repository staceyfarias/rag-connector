# Transit times by leg — Pelorus Voyages

Pelorus Voyages vessels use continuous-thrust fusion drives that maintain a sustained 0.15g acceleration for the first half of each leg and decelerate at 0.15g for the second half. This profile makes transit times measured in days rather than months and produces a low but constant gravity sensation throughout the journey — beneficial for passenger comfort and health. Times below are typical values for departure windows during planned alignment periods; alignment affects fuel efficiency but not the drive's capability, so variance is lower than on Hohmann-trajectory vessels.

| Leg | Typical transit | One-way light delay at arrival | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earth → Moon | 2 days | 1.3 seconds | All itineraries. Short by design; Moon Base 7 is the staging hub. |
| Moon → Mars | 4 days | 3–22 minutes (orbital position dependent) | IPS and GST. Departure alignment still matters for fuel efficiency; Pelorus schedules around optimal windows. |
| Mars → Venus | 3 days | 2–15 minutes (orbital position dependent) | IPS and GST only. |
| Moon → Ceres (ORT outbound) | 4 days | 4–20 minutes | ORT only. Direct from Moon Base 7 after layover. |
| Venus → Ceres (GST) | 4 days | 4–20 minutes | GST only. Leg connecting Inner Planets to Outer Reaches sequence. |
| Ceres → Saturn | 5 days | 43–84 minutes (orbital position dependent) | ORT and GST. Longest single leg; Saturn's position in its 29-year orbit affects light delay significantly. |
| Saturn → Titan | 8 hours | ~4 seconds additional from Saturn | Short hop; Titan orbits Saturn at 1.2 million km. |
| Titan → Enceladus | 20 hours | ~1 second additional | Enceladus orbits inside Titan; brief backtrack. |
| Enceladus → Earth (return) | 8–9 days | 43–84 minutes | Return arc is separately optimised; does not retrace the outbound route. |
| Venus → Earth (IPS return) | 2 days | 2–15 minutes | |
| Mars → Earth (IPS alternative return, rare) | 3 days | 3–22 minutes | |

## Communication delays

Light delay determines one-way message travel time. Round-trip (question and answer) is twice the one-way delay. At Mars, expect 6–44 minutes for a reply. At Saturn and beyond, expect 86–168 minutes. Real-time conversation is only practical within the Moon's orbit. All ship-to-Earth communication beyond that is asynchronous. The vessel operates a relay queue; messages are transmitted in batches at scheduled windows.

## Total voyage durations

| Itinerary | Total duration | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Inner Planets Survey (IPS) | 18 days | Earth-Moon transit (2d) + Moon layover (1d) + Moon-Mars transit (4d) + Mars stay (3 nights) + Mars-Venus transit (3d) + Venus stay (3 nights) + Venus-Earth return (2d) |
| Outer Reaches Tour (ORT) | 24 days | Earth-Moon transit (2d) + Moon layover (1d) + Moon-Ceres transit (4d) + Ceres stop (6h) + Ceres-Saturn transit (5d) + Ring transit (6h) + Saturn-Titan (8h) + Titan excursion (8h) + Titan-Enceladus (20h) + Enceladus flyover (4h) + Enceladus-Earth return (8d) |
| Grand Solar Tour (GST) | 38 days | All IPS stops + Venus-Ceres transit (4d) + all ORT stops from Ceres onward |

Note: days are Earth standard days. The vessel's internal clock runs on UTC. Destinations use their own local time conventions for excursion scheduling; all muster times in the voyage manifest are given in UTC.
